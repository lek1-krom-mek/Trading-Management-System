§ 03 · KELLY MATHEMATICS

Why the textbook Kelly fraction is useless — and what to use instead.

The classical Kelly fraction for a binary bet with win probability p, loss probability q = 1 − p, and payoff ratio b is:

f* = (b · p − q) / b   =   p − q/b

For a 55% win rate across the SOP's R:R grid:

R:R (b) Full Kelly f* Half Kelly Quarter Kelly EV / trade (R) σ / trade (R) Sharpe / trade
1:1.5 25.00% 12.50% 6.25% 0.375 1.244 0.302
1:2.0 32.50% 16.25% 8.125% 0.650 1.493 0.436
1:2.5 37.00% 18.50% 9.25% 0.925 1.741 0.531
1:3.0 40.00% 20.00% 10.00% 1.200 1.990 0.603
1:4.0 43.75% 21.88% 10.94% 1.750 2.488 0.703
Kelly fractions and trade-level statistics at 55% win rate.

Full Kelly at 1:2R implies risking nearly a third of equity per trade. Thorp's log-normal result shows the expected drawdown of a full-Kelly bettor is ~50% of peak equity. Even quarter Kelly (8.125% at 1:2R) would breach HolaPrime's 8% overall drawdown in a single losing trade. Every prop-firm risk level in our grid (0.25%–2%) is already deeply sub-Kelly — between 1/130th and 1/16th of full Kelly.

So the correct question isn't "what fraction of Kelly?" — it's what is the highest risk that keeps the probability of touching the drawdown barrier acceptably low?

§ 04 · BREACH PROBABILITY

The binding constraint is drawdown, not ruin. Here is the heatmap.

Prop-firm "ruin" isn't a 100% wipeout — it's hitting the 8% static barrier. The classical Kaufman ruin formula gives essentially zero steady-state ruin probability at any sane risk level, but understates prop-firm risk because it measures eventual ruin, not the probability the random-walk path touches 8% somewhere along the way.

The correct tool is the Magdon-Ismail expected-maximum-drawdown formula:

E[MaxDD] ≈ (σ² / μ) · ln(N)   in R-units

For a 55%/2R system, σ²/μ = 3.427 R. Computed across 100 trades (≈ a two-month challenge or funded cycle):

Goal 3 · Risk-adjusted (Kelly / Sharpe)

Two practical risk-adjusted rankings emerge:

  • Geometric growth per trade (Kelly-native metric): at 0.5% risk, g/trade = p·ln(1+bp) + q·ln(1−r) — best at 1:3 R:R (g ≈ 0.57%/trade). Peaks near 1:3 because the (b+1)² variance penalty compounds at higher R:R.
  • Growth-to-barrier ratio: EV%/trade divided by 90th-percentile MDD. Best at 0.5% risk, 1:2.5 or 1:3 R:R, giving a ratio near 0.07/trade.

Half-Kelly would prescribe 16.25% (at 1:2R) — 32× too aggressive for an 8% barrier. The barrier-adjusted Kelly, computed as Kelly × (barrier / expected full-Kelly MDD) ≈ 32.5% × (8%/50%) ≈ 5.2%, then discounted ~10× for estimation error in the 55% WR assumption, lands at 0.5–0.65% — exactly where both the speed and breach analyses independently converge.

§ 06 · POSITION SIZING FOR GOLD

Translating % risk into actual lot sizes for XAU/USD.

Using the MT5 prop-firm convention ($10 per pip per 1.0 standard lot where a "pip" means $0.10 price move), the formula is:

Lot size = Dollar risk / (Stop in pips × Pip value)

Gold's NY-killzone 5-minute order-block setups genuinely need 30–50 pips of wiggle room — anything tighter donates to stop-hunters given XAU's typical intraday noise of $0.80–$1.50 ATR on 5M. The 30-pip stop is the realistic planning number.

§ 09 · HARD RULES

Six commandments the math demands, not opinions.

  • Never exceed 1.5% risk on any account, ever. The daily-breach math makes higher risk a 9% single-day coin flip for termination.
  • Never use R:R below 1:2 — SOP minimum confirmed. Sharpe at 1:1.5 is 30% lower than at 1:2; EV is 42% lower.
  • Never use full, half, or even quarter Kelly. The prop-firm barrier distorts the entire Kelly framework. Correct fraction is ~1.5% of full Kelly.
  • Target two trades per day, not three. This preserves LLW/LWL paths as non-breaches and keeps worst intraday DD at 2R instead of 3R. Cuts daily breach probability roughly in half.
  • Stop trading for the day at −2R intraday. This hard rule keeps daily drawdown ≤ 1% regardless of risk level and eliminates tail-day breaches entirely.
  • Scale account size, not risk percentage. Buying a $100K challenge at 0.5% risk beats pushing a $10K to 1% risk on every metric — speed, safety, and risk-adjusted.

Every independent analytical route — Kelly discounted for the drawdown barrier, Magdon-Ismail expected max drawdown, binomial daily-breach enumeration, Sharpe subject to the barrier, industry pass-rate data — lands in the same narrow corridor: 0.4–0.5% risk per trade, 1:2 to 1:2.5 R:R, two trades per day, a −2R daily stop-trading rule, and aggressive scaling of account size rather than risk percentage when profits allow.
